    For teachers

    Project Scroll for classrooms

    New: the teachers’ pack. A one-day field trip to Cassopolis (the Broadway walk, the Local History Branch and the photo spots), with two printable worksheets: rephotographing an old postcard, and reading an old record. Download the teachers’ pack (PDF)

    Four ready lessons in local history and map reading, built on the real maps, directories and county histories of the St. Joseph River Valley. Free, nothing to sign up for, and nothing collected from students. Print this page and go.

    Then and now

    Grades 3 and up · 40 minutes · Map reading, change over time

    You need: Seven towns, then and now and a screen or printed copies.

    1. Choose a town near your school. Open its page and fade slowly from the old plan to today.
    2. List three things that are still there (a street, a square, a river crossing) and three that are gone.
    3. Find the street that changed its name, using the challenge on the page.
    4. Talk about it: why might a town rename a street? Who decides?

    Going further: Walk the street, if you can, with the “Where am I?” button on a phone, and photograph what stands there now.

    Who was in town

    Grades 4 to 8 · 45 minutes · Primary sources as data, graphs

    You need: Who was in town and a screen or printed copies.

    1. Pick one town’s 1860 or 1861 business directory.
    2. Sort the entries into trades: law, medicine, food, building, transport, making things.
    3. Make a bar graph of the trades. Which town had the most lawyers? The most shoemakers?
    4. Ask: who is missing from a directory that people paid to be listed in? (Most farmers, labourers, women and children.)

    Going further: Compare two towns, or a directory of today, and write what changed.

    This day in the valley

    Grades 6 to 12 · 45 minutes · Primary and secondary sources, citation

    You need: The valley’s almanac and a screen or printed copies.

    1. Find the almanac entry nearest to today’s date, or a student’s birthday.
    2. Open the county history page it cites. Read the whole paragraph around the date.
    3. Decide: was the writer there? How many years after the event did they write? Who told them?
    4. Write a one-sentence citation for the source, with book, year and page.

    Going further: Look for the same event in a newspaper of the time and compare the two accounts.

    Weighing the evidence

    Grades 6 to 12 · 50 minutes · Evaluating sources, reasoning from evidence

    You need: Reading gravestones and the death certificate guide and a screen or printed copies.

    1. Read the guide together. List who made a gravestone, a death certificate and a county history, and when.
    2. Give pairs a disagreement: an age on a stone and an age on a certificate that differ by a year.
    3. Each pair decides which to trust and writes why, using the guide’s questions.
    4. Share: what extra record would settle it?

    Going further: Play one of the open cases on the case board as a class, and post the class’s answer.

    Notes for teachers

    • Every source is public domain and credited where it is held. Students can open the original every time.
    • The junior hunt, seven questions answered from the maps and directories, makes a good warm-up: Junior surveyors.
    • Nothing to dig, nowhere to trespass. Every answer is in a book, a map or a record. Please teach the same rule on any field trip.
    • Ideas, corrections, or a class that solved a case? Write to research@projectscroll.com.
