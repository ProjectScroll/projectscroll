    Eagle Lake

    Band night at French’s Hotel, Eagle Lake

    2 min read

    A brass band on folding chairs under the trees, a flag strung overhead, and buggies and motor cars drawn up behind. A summer evening at Eagle Lake, on a card of 1912.

    Project Scroll · 22 October 2026 · 2 min read

    Edwardsburg, Mich. The band sits in a ring of folding chairs on the bare ground under the trees. The players wear peaked caps, their music is clipped to stands, and the trombones and the big bass drum stand out among the brass. A flag is strung on a line overhead. Around them the crowd stands close: men in shirtsleeves and straw hats, women in white, and in front one man grinning at the camera with a cigar in his teeth. Behind them are horses and a buggy, and motor cars with their tops up.

    DocumentedThe words are written across the top of the card: “Band Concert, French’s Hotel, Eagle Lake, Mich.” It is a real-photo postcard of 1912, now in the David V. Tinder Collection at the University of Michigan. No photographer’s name appears on its face.

    French’s

    DocumentedEagle Lake is in Ontwa Township, Edwardsburg’s own. The village’s history, as the Edwardsburg Chamber tells it, says that in summer “city folks detrained in the village” and were “driven by horse and carriage to French’s Resort on Eagle Lake.”

    The Edwardsburg Area History Museum has gathered the resort’s life in postcards, many of them provided by Floyd Jerdon of Dowagiac. Its gallery has French’s Landing about 1901 and in 1907, French’s Hotel in 1910 and 1911, another band concert at the resort in 1917, and “Hotel French’s, Eagle Lake Resort” still on a card of 1941. The Eagle Lake Sailing Club says it had its “early beginnings in 1936 at French’s Hotel.”

    Open questions

    OpenWhich band is this: Edwardsburg’s own, a band from Elkhart or South Bend, or the resort’s? Who was the French of French’s, when did the hotel open, and when did it close? Can you name anyone in the crowd? If you know, or if the band played in your family, write to research@projectscroll.com. The museum on Main Street in Edwardsburg is the place to see the rest of the cards.
