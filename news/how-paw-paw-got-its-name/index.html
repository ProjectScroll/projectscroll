    How Paw Paw got its name

    Series · 5 of 7 ›Go see it7 min read

    Paw Paw is named for its river, and the river for the pawpaw tree. But “pawpaw” is not a Native word, the village was laid out as Paw Paw, not Lafayette, and the first Paw Paw in local government was in the next county. We went back to the record.

    By Michael Urbanski · Project Scroll · 22 October 2026 · 7 min read

    Paw Paw, Mich. Paw Paw takes its name from the Paw Paw River, and the river from the pawpaw, the small native tree with the big tropical-tasting fruit that grew, the county history says, thickly along its banks. So far, so simple. But the county history says the river was “named by the Indians,” and pawpaw is an English word; many people believe the village was first called Lafayette, though it was laid out as Paw Paw; and the first place in local government to be called Paw Paw was in Berrien County.

    We went back to the county history of 1880, the federal place-names list of 1905, the state’s historical marker at the courthouse and the county map of 1860 to see what the record shows.

    At a glance

    The place
    Paw Paw, Michigan: the seat of Van Buren County, at the forks of the Paw Paw River
    Laid out
    By the mill-owners Peter Gremps and Lyman J. Daniels, who bought the site in 1833: “a village which they called Paw Paw”
    Post office
    1835, with Peter Gremps as postmaster
    County seat
    1837, chosen by the supervisors over a governor’s commission’s choice of Lawrence
    The township
    Lafayette from 1835; renamed Paw Paw on 15 March 1867
    First “Paw Paw”
    A Berrien County township of 7 March 1831, now Watervliet and Hagar townships
    Why it matters
    The village kept its river’s name all along; it was the township that was called Lafayette

    The case in brief

    What the record shows

    • The village was named for the river: the founders laid out “a village which they called Paw Paw.”
    • Lafayette was the township, from 1835, not the village. The township took the village’s name on 15 March 1867.
    • The post office opened in 1835 under Peter Gremps; the county seat came in 1837.
    • The first civil “Paw Paw” was in Berrien County, a township erected in 1831 on the same river.

    What it does not show, yet

    • Any Native-language name of the river, in a published source.
    • When the village plat was recorded, and when the village was incorporated: 1837 and 1867 are both given.
    • Why the township was called Lafayette.

    Story or record? Play along

    Here are eight things people say about the name. For each, make your call: true, false, or no record either way. Then see what the paper shows.

    “Paw Paw takes its name from the Paw Paw River.”

    True

    The 1880 county history says the township was named from the village in 1867, and the village “was itself called after the river.”

    “The village of Paw Paw was first called Lafayette.”

    No

    Lafayette was the township, from 1835 to 1867. The founders “laid out … a village which they called Paw Paw,” and the county-seat papers of 1837 say “village of Paw Paw.”

    “The township changed its name from Lafayette to Paw Paw in 1867.”

    True

    “The name of the township of Lafayette was changed to Paw Paw on the 15th of March, 1867,” says the 1880 history.

    “‘Pawpaw’ is a Potawatomi word.”

    No

    It is an English word, most likely from the Spanish papaya, and was used for the North American tree by about 1760.

    “The Indians named the river for the pawpaw fruit.”

    No record

    The 1880 history says so, but it prints no Native-language name for the river, and we have not found one in any published source.

    “Peter Gremps was Paw Paw’s first postmaster.”

    True

    “Peter Gremps was the first postmaster of Paw Paw, his appointment dating from 1835.” A street on the 1860 map carries his name.

    “The state first picked Lawrence, not Paw Paw, as county seat.”

    True

    The state’s historical marker at the courthouse says that in 1837 “a gubernatorial commission chose Lawrence as the seat, but the board of supervisors chose Paw Paw.”

    “The first Paw Paw in local government was in Van Buren County.”

    No

    Berrien County had a “township of Paw Paw … erected March 7, 1831,” now Watervliet and Hagar townships, four years before Van Buren’s Paw Paw post office.

    The river and the tree

    DocumentedThe 1880 county history says the township “was named from” the village in 1867, and the village “was itself called after the river, named by the Indians from the paw paw fruit, growing thickly upon its banks.” Henry Gannett’s federal Origin of Certain Place Names (1905) agrees, writing it as one word: Pawpaw, named for the “presence of pawpaw trees.”

    ReadingThe tree is real enough. The pawpaw, Asimina triloba, grows wild in southwest Michigan, near the northern edge of its range. But “pawpaw” is an English word, most likely from the Spanish papaya, first used for the tropical fruit and applied to this tree by about 1760. So the river’s English name may translate a Native one; it cannot be one.

    OpenThe history prints no Native-language name for the river, and we have found none in a published source. If the river had one, it is still to be found.

    A village which they called Paw Paw

    DocumentedPeter Gremps and Lyman J. Daniels bought the mill property at the forks of the river in 1833, the 1880 history says, and “laid out upon it a village which they called Paw Paw.” A settler named Rodney Hinckley had taken up a farm on the north side of the future village in 1832.

    Documented“Peter Gremps was the first postmaster of Paw Paw, his appointment dating from 1835.” The county’s supervisors met “at the village of Paw Paw on the 27th day of March, A.D. 1837,” and the seat of justice was located “in the village of Paw Paw.”

    DocumentedThe state’s historical marker at the courthouse adds that in 1837 “a gubernatorial commission chose Lawrence as the seat, but the board of supervisors chose Paw Paw,” and that the county’s voters kept the seat in Paw Paw on 1 April 1901.

    The township called Lafayette

    DocumentedIn 1835 the Legislative Council formed the whole of Van Buren County into one township, called Lafayette. When the county was divided in 1837, “the present Paw Paw retained the old name of Lafayette.” Then: “The name of the township of Lafayette was changed to Paw Paw on the 15th of March, 1867.”

    ReadingSo for thirty years the village of Paw Paw stood in the township of Lafayette, and the two names were easily confused, even at the time: an early court paper is returnable “at the court-house in Lafayette.” That is almost certainly where the belief that the village was first called Lafayette comes from.

    OpenWhy Lafayette? The Marquis de Lafayette’s tour of America in 1824–25 made his name popular for new towns, but the history does not say, and we have found no record of the choice.

    The other Paw Paw

    DocumentedThe first Paw Paw in local government was not here. The 1880 history records that “the township of Paw Paw was erected March 7, 1831,” in Berrien County, on the lower river, on the ground of today’s Watervliet and Hagar townships. That was four years before the Van Buren County post office.

    The name on paper

    Play the names in order, from the river township of 1831 to the town today.

    • 1831 Paw Paw township (Berrien County, now Watervliet and Hagar)
    • 1833 Forks of the Paw Paw (a road act, as the 1880 history reports it)
    • 1835 Paw Paw (the post office)
    • 1835 Lafayette (the township, Van Buren County)
    • 1837 village of Paw Paw (the county-seat papers)
    • 1867 Lafayette becomes Paw Paw (the township renamed)
    • 1905 Pawpaw (Gannett, as one word)

    Open questions

    These are the questions we cannot yet answer. If you can help with any of them, please write to us.

    1. Did the river have a Native name? A published vocabulary or an early traveller’s account may hold it.
    2. When was the village plat recorded? The Van Buren County register of deeds would say.
    3. When was the village incorporated? The 1880 history says the spring of 1867; other summaries say 1837. The session laws will settle it.
    4. Why Lafayette?
