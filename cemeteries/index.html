    Family research, county by county

    Find a grave or an obituary

    Where the burial records, obituary indexes and death certificates are for the seven counties of the St. Joseph River valley, and how to ask for a copy. Each entry links to the office or library that holds the record. Fees and hours change: check before you write or go.

    Cass County, Michigan · Berrien County, Michigan · Van Buren County, Michigan · St. Joseph County, Michigan · St. Joseph County, Indiana · Elkhart County, Indiana · LaPorte County, Indiana

    Cass County, Michigan

    Burials and cemetery records

    • Township and village clerks / sextons. Most Cass County cemeteries are owned by the townships (or the village/city); burial and lot records sit with the township clerk or sexton. The Cass County MIGenWeb page notes that township officials, not volunteers, handle lots. Reach a township through its own office (township halls are listed on the county website). Example: Prospect Hill Cemetery in Cassopolis is owned and operated by the Village of Cassopolis, (269) 445-8648, manager@cassopolis-mi.us.
    • Cass District Library, Local History Branch - cemetery indexes. Holds the DAR (Capt. Samuel Felt Chapter) index of Cass County cemeteries made in the late 1930s-early 1940s, plus published township/cemetery indexes (e.g., Silver Creek Twp. 1994, Wayne Twp. 1994, Jefferson Twp. 1996, Young's Prairie 1996 with burial permits and sexton records). Printed, viewed at the branch (145 N. Broadway, Cassopolis; Mon-Thu 9-4; (269) 357-7823). Research guide PDF online; a second PDF lists additional indexes.
    • Cass District Library - additional cemetery indexes list. PDF list of further Cass County cemetery indexes in the Local History Branch.
    • DAR 'Cass County, Michigan cemetery records' (Capt. Samuel Felt Chapter). Compiled DAR cemetery readings; on microfilm at the Library of Michigan, Lansing (call no. F574.O847 C4 1980a), per the Library of Michigan Cass County guide.
    • Cass County MIGenWeb - Cemeteries. Volunteer index of about 71 cemeteries across 15 townships, marked active/retired/abandoned; most link to burial listings drawn from 1940s DAR readings and later volunteer readings; also sells transcribed cemetery books. Site warns listings may be incomplete.
    • MIGenWeb Tombstone Photo Project - Cass County. Volunteer headstone photographs by cemetery (e.g., Prospect Hill, Cassopolis).
    • Library of Michigan - cemetery transcripts. The state library in Lansing holds cemetery transcripts for Michigan counties among its family-history collections.
    • Find a Grave / BillionGraves. Both volunteer memorial/headstone databases cover Cass County cemeteries.
    • Interment.net - Michigan. Free index of cemetery transcriptions by Michigan county; check for Cass County entries.
    • VA Nationwide Gravesite Locator. U.S. Dept. of Veterans Affairs search for burial places of veterans and family members in national, state veterans' and some other cemeteries; last name required.

    Obituaries

    • Cass District Library - Local Newspaper Obituary Indexes. PDF name indexes to obituaries in local papers (file names cite the Cassopolis Vigilant, Edwardsburg Argus and Marcellus News). Years: 1998-2012 (one combined file; its filename says 2008-2012) then one PDF per year 2013-2025 How to get a copy: Indexes are free online; the papers themselves are on microfilm at the Local History Branch, 145 N. Broadway, Cassopolis. No copy-request form or fee is posted - call (269) 357-7823 or (866) 808-7323, or email the branch through the address on its page.
    • Southwest Michigan Obituary Index (Van Buren Regional Genealogical Society). Name index of over 89,000 obituaries for Allegan, Berrien, Cass, Kalamazoo and Van Buren counties, citing the Herald-Palladium, Kalamazoo Gazette, South Bend Tribune, Niles Daily Star, funeral records and other sources; each entry gives date, source code and page. Years: earliest history through 2000 How to get a copy: By mail: $3.00 per item (check, cash or money order) with a self-addressed stamped envelope and the full index entry, to VBRGS, P.O. Box 143, Decatur, MI 49045 (terms as posted on the index page; confirm before sending).
    • Cass District Library - Connelly Funeral Home records. Year-by-year PDF listings of funeral home records. Years: 1917-1930 How to get a copy: Free PDFs online; originals at the Local History Branch.
    • Cassopolis Vigilant microfilm guide. Research guide to the library's microfilm reels of the Cassopolis Vigilant. Years: see guide How to get a copy: Use at the Local History Branch.
    • Library of Michigan newspaper microfilm. Cassopolis Vigilant 1872-2016, Dowagiac Times 1887-1906, Edwardsburg Argus 1879-2016, Marcellus News 1887-2015 on microfilm in Lansing. Years: 1872-2016 (varies by title) How to get a copy: Visit in Lansing or ask via Librarian@Michigan.gov / 517-335-1477.
    • Chronicling America - Cass County Republican (Dowagiac). Free digitized newspaper, listed by the Clarke Historical Library's Michigan newspaper portal. Years: 1858-1880 How to get a copy: Free online.
    • Digital Michigan Newspaper Portal (Clarke Historical Library, CMU). County-by-county index of digitized Michigan papers, each marked Free/Pay/Hybrid. For Cass it lists the Cass County Republican (free) and the Cassopolis Vigilant, Dowagiac Daily News and Edwardsburg Argus 2009-current (pay, NewsBank). Years: varies How to get a copy: Online.

    Death records

    • Michigan death certificates on Michiganology. The Library of Michigan says Michiganology has Michigan death certificates 1897-1946, indexed to 1952. The Archives of Michigan collection page describes about 1.6 million records with images for 1921-1948 and index only for 1949-1952, so check coverage for the year you need.
    • Library of Michigan - vital records research guide. Explains Michigan death indexes (1867-1914), returns (1867-1897), certificates (1897-1920 on microfilm; 1897-1952 on Ancestry), and the state Division of Vital Records, P.O. Box 30721, Lansing MI 48909, 517-335-8666.
    • Cass County death registers (microfilm at the Local History Branch). Cass County death records 1867 to July 2005 filmed at the courthouse: indexes 1867-1960 and 1960-2005; Libers A and B 1867-1927; Liber C 1926-1940; volumes 1934-2005. No death records were kept 1829-1866.
    • Cass County Clerk/Register. Registrar of vital records; issues death certificates and genealogy copies (fees not posted; online ordering via a third-party portal). Phone (269) 445-4464.

    Old cemeteries you can walk

    • Prospect Hill Cemetery, Disbrow Street, Cassopolis, MI 49031 (village-owned). The county seat's cemetery; first recorded burial 7 August 1832, incorporated 1879; maps are posted by the village, and volunteers flag veterans' graves for Memorial Day. More

    Berrien County, Michigan

    Burials and cemetery records

    • Township and city cemetery offices. Berrien County cemeteries are mostly township- or city-owned; ask the township clerk/sexton or city office that owns the ground. Examples: Silverbrook Cemetery (City of Niles) office 269-683-8421; MIGenWeb notes Highland Cemetery belongs to the City of St. Joseph.
    • Berrien County Genealogical Society (BCGS) - cemetery records. Online cemetery list of 106 county cemeteries by township. 'All Berrien County Interments' and Calvary Cemetery (Niles Twp.) are free to view; most other cemetery records are members-only. BCGS also has a store and downloadable files; printed titles and prices are not listed on the pages read. Contact info@bcgensoc.com.
    • Berrien County MIGenWeb - Cemetery Records. Volunteer index of about 92 cemeteries by township with readings and photos (partial for some), contributors including William Brackett and Deanna West; also links the Michigan Tombstone Project for Berrien.
    • Friends of Silverbrook - Grave Search. Searchable burial, photo and obituary search for Silverbrook Cemetery, Niles.
    • Michiana Genealogical Index (South Bend Area Genealogical Society). Free surname index (compiled by Norbert Cramer over 30+ years) of births, deaths, marriages and cemetery sources from Michiana-area records, pointing to where the record is; MIGenWeb's Berrien pages refer to it for cemetery listings.
    • Index to Cemetery Records, Berrien County (Mary Jane Trout, 1983). Index of persons born before 1867 in Berrien cemetery records; at the Library of Michigan (F572.B5 C454 1930 Index).
    • Library of Michigan - cemetery transcripts. The state library in Lansing holds cemetery transcripts for Michigan counties among its family-history collections.
    • Find a Grave / BillionGraves. Both volunteer memorial/headstone databases cover the county's cemeteries.
    • Interment.net - Michigan. Free index of cemetery transcriptions by Michigan county; check for this county's entries.
    • VA Nationwide Gravesite Locator. U.S. Dept. of Veterans Affairs search for burial places of veterans and family members in national, state veterans' and some other cemeteries; last name required.

    • St. Joseph / Benton Harbor dailies (pay sites). Herald-Press (St. Joseph) 1901-1975 on Newspapers.com; News-Palladium (Benton Harbor) 1904-1975 on NewspaperArchive, per the Clarke portal.     Old cemeteries you can walk

    • Silverbrook Cemetery, 1400 E. Main St., Niles, MI 49120 (gates at Cherry and Main). Niles's public cemetery since 1836 (George Bond's six acres, lots sold from 1838), now over 60 acres; 1926 gates, abbey and chime tower; burials include Calvin F. Wilson, a charter member of Michigan's first Black Masonic lodge, and the Plym family of Kawneer. Friends of Silverbrook and the Niles History Center run an annual tour (4 Oct 2026) and a virtual tour. More
    • Crystal Springs Cemetery, Napier Avenue (between Pipestone Rd. and Crystal Ave.), Benton Township, near Benton Harbor. Founded in 1893 by the City of Benton Harbor; grew from 30 to over 110 acres, landscaped under superintendent Herman Vogt (1921-53), with the Tower of Remembrance bell tower; Calvary (Catholic) and a Jewish cemetery adjoin it. More

    Van Buren County, Michigan

    Burials and cemetery records

    • Township and city cemetery offices. Van Buren cemeteries are largely township- or city-owned. Paw Paw Township posts alphabetical occupant lists (PDF) and maps for Cuddeback, Prospect Hill and Wildey cemeteries; Hartford Township and the City of South Haven have cemetery pages; all are linked from the MIGenWeb cemetery index.
    • Van Buren County MIGenWeb - Cemeteries. About 60 cemeteries across 18 townships with locations; about 16 have readings or photos on the site, others link to USGenWeb tombstone images.
    • Van Buren District Library - Toni I. Benson Room of Local History and Genealogy. Holds 'cemetery books for Southwest Michigan' (contents not described online) and the VBRGS holdings. Webster Memorial Library, 200 N. Phelps St., Decatur; Mon-Fri 10-4, Sat 9-3; 269-423-4771.
    • Van Buren Regional Genealogical Society (VBRGS). Collection housed at the Hartford Public Library, 12 Church St., Hartford; in-house use, free to the public; volunteers help by appointment (VBRGS@yahoo.com). No cemetery publications for sale are listed on its site.
    • Library of Michigan - cemetery transcripts. The state library in Lansing holds cemetery transcripts for Michigan counties among its family-history collections.
    • Find a Grave / BillionGraves. Both volunteer memorial/headstone databases cover the county's cemeteries.
    • Interment.net - Michigan. Free index of cemetery transcriptions by Michigan county; check for this county's entries.

    Burials and cemetery records

    • Township and city clerks / cemetery offices. St. Joseph County (MI) cemeteries are mostly township- or city-owned. MIGenWeb keeps a list of township and city clerks with addresses and phones; its cemetery list was supplied by the County Clerk's office. Example: City of Sturgis cemetery division (Oak Lawn and Memorial Gardens), Cemetery and Parks Supervisor at City Hall.

    • St. Joseph County Historical Society archives. Three Rivers archive of original records, obituaries, wills, land records and directories, with archivists to help. 34 N. Main St., Three Rivers; Fridays 10-4 or by appointment; (269) 273-6003; SJCHS34@gmail.com. MIGenWeb lists it as the county's genealogical society.

    St. Joseph County, Indiana

    Burials and cemetery records

    • City and township cemetery offices. Indiana township trustees and city governments own many cemeteries. South Bend's City Cemetery (and Bowman Cemetery) are run by South Bend Venues Parks & Arts, 219 S. St. Louis Blvd., 574-299-4765, which posts an interactive City Cemetery map (in development).
    • South Bend Area Genealogical Society (SBAGS) - cemetery databases. Cemetery Locations in St. Joseph County (public; township, legal description, coordinates, directions, notes on lost stones); Cemetery Inscriptions Index (members only); Burial Permits Index 1910-1929; Graves Registration Project (county veterans who died before 1938).
    • SBAGS Graves Registration Project index (RootsWeb). Free text/PDF index of St. Joseph County male veterans who died before 1938/1939 with a cemetery code key; from SBAGS's printed 'Index to Graves Registration Project Form GR5', at the St. Joseph County Public Library.
    • St. Joseph County INGenWeb - cemeteries. Transcribed cemeteries, links to the SBAGS inscription index, Cedar Grove and old-section St. Joseph (Polish) inscriptions, and a combined spreadsheet.
    • Interment.net - St. Joseph County, Indiana. Free cemetery transcription index for the county.
    • Indiana DNR Cemetery & Burial Ground Registry (CBGR). State registry of all Indiana cemeteries and burial grounds kept by the Division of Historic Preservation & Archaeology: location, marker count, condition, affiliations, veterans, features (not a list of names). Searchable inside SHAARD and shown on the IHBBC map.
    • Indiana State Library - Cemetery Locator Index. Statewide catalog of Indiana cemeteries by county and township with legal location and the State Library call numbers of books that list each one.
    • Find a Grave / BillionGraves. Both volunteer memorial/headstone databases cover the county's cemeteries.
    • VA Nationwide Gravesite Locator. U.S. Dept. of Veterans Affairs search for burial places of veterans and family members in national, state veterans' and some other cemeteries; last name required.

    Death records

    • Indiana death certificates 1899-2011 (Ancestry). Statewide death certificates with images and index, filed 1899-2011, from Indiana State Board of Health records on microfilm at the Indiana Archives and Records Administration. The Indiana State Archives says unofficial copies 'may be obtained on Ancestry.com'; the Indiana State Library lists it as available free in the library and statewide via Ancestry Library Edition.
    • Indiana State Archives (IARA) - vital records guidance. For 1882-1906 deaths, contact the county health office where the death occurred; 1907 and later from the Indiana Department of Health (orderable online). The Indiana State Library Genealogy Division holds WPA death indexes for about half the counties.
    • Indiana Death Index 1882-1920 (FamilySearch). Free index covering 67 of 92 counties, per the Indiana State Library.
    • SBAGS Coroner's Records Index 1879-1960. Index to St. Joseph County coroner's records.

    Old cemeteries you can walk

    • South Bend City Cemetery, 214 Elm Street (at Colfax), South Bend, IN 46616. South Bend's oldest cemetery (land given by Lathrop Taylor and Alexis Coquillard; first burial 1831), 21 acres, about 14,800 burials, listed on the National Register in 2018. Graves include Vice President Schuyler Colfax, the Studebaker brothers (Clement, John, Peter), President McKinley's paternal grandparents, and Medal of Honor recipient Enoch R. Weiss. More

    Burials and cemetery records

Runs Rice (400 James St.), Grace Lawn and Prairie Street cemeteries; helps locate hard-to-find burials and handles genealogy requests; holds the list of people moved from the old Middlebury Street cemetery.     • City of Goshen Department of Cemeteries - Goshen Cemetery Explorer.     Old cemeteries you can walk

    • Grace Lawn Cemetery, Elkhart, IN (city-owned; street address not on the city page). Dedicated in 1864 and named for Grace Clark, foster daughter of town founder-surveyor Charles Beardsley; early Elkhart families, over 100 Civil War soldiers, burials moved from the old Middlebury Street cemetery, a 1918 gateway and the Baldwin-Beardsley mausoleum. More
    • Oakridge Cemetery, 435 N. 1st Street, Goshen, IN. Burials from 1832, city-owned since 1859, over 40 acres; mayors, Goshen's first postmaster, veterans of every major American war, and GAR, Odd Fellows and Jewish (Sharis Israel) sections. More

    Good manners in old cemeteries

    • Visit in daylight, keep to the paths, and leave everything as you found it.
    • Never rub, chalk or clean a stone; photograph it in raking light instead. Our gravestone guide shows how.
    • Township cemeteries belong to the township: the clerk or sexton keeps the lot records.
