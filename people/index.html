    Find your people

    Who were they, and where is it written?

    Everyone who lived in the valley left a trail of paper: a census line, a deed, a notice in the weekly paper, a name on a stone. Answer three questions and the record finder lists where to look first, on this site and in the free collections, and which county office keeps the books.

    The record finder

      Nothing you type is sent anywhere unless you share the link yourself; a shared link carries your three answers and the surname, if you typed one. The page’s own address never holds the surname. The list is a starting order, not a guarantee: records were lost, burned or never kept in some places and years. The records page has the rest.

      Three habits that find more

      • Spell it every way. Clerks wrote what they heard. Try the name as it sounds.
      • Read the neighbours. The families on either side in a census are often in-laws; the witnesses to a deed are often kin.
      • Go back to the original. An index finds the record. The record itself is the evidence.
