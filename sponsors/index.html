    Keeping the hunt free

    Sponsor the hunt

    The hunt is free to play and always will be. The research behind it is not free to do: the scans, the certificates, the microfilm, the hours in the record. Sponsors pay for those. In return they are named, thanked, and shown exactly what their money bought.

    What sponsorship buys, and what it never buys

    Sponsorship funds the work. It does not choose a case, shape a finding, or see a report before readers do. No sponsor’s name goes beside evidence. If a sponsor ever asked for any of that, the money would go back. That is the whole bargain, and it is why a sponsor’s name on this site means something.

    Four ways in

    Individuals

    Friend of the hunt

    Any amount, once or monthly

    • Named on this page (or anonymous, as you prefer)
    • The Benefactor tin badge in your field book
    • Thanked in the feed when a case you helped fund is solved

    Most friends give $5 a month or $25 once. Every dollar goes to the record.

    Become a friend

    Individuals and families

    Patron of a case

    $150 · one case

    • Underwrites the next case on the board: research hours, scans, certificates
    • Named on that case’s page: “Opened with the help of …”
    • A copy of the sourced report when the case closes
    • The Patron badge

    Patrons fund the next case in line. They do not choose it, and they never choose the answer.

    Fund a case

    Businesses

    Business sponsor

    $300 a year · three places

    • A card in the sponsors’ strip on the home page: your name, one line, a link
    • Named on this page and in the yearly ledger
    • Thanked once a quarter on Project Scroll’s Facebook page
    • Never placed beside a case’s evidence, never in a post

    Set like the business cards in an old county newspaper. Three places only, so each is worth having.

    Ask about a place

    Organizations

    Underwriter

    $1,000 and up · a season

    • Underwrites a whole hall of the hunt, a document series, or a year of the Document of the Month
    • “Presented with …” on the hall’s page and in the feed
    • A short letter of agreement, and the ledger line that shows what it paid for

    For a library, a historical society, a lake association, a family reunion, a foundation. Research to your own question is a commission, not a sponsorship: the research desk.

    Talk to us

    Other ways to keep it going

    • Give a document. A scan of a county history, an atlas, a letter, a photograph of a place as it was. Credited by name on the page it opens.
    • Give a record. Buy one certificate or one microfilm reel for a case and we post the receipt in the ledger beside your name.
    • Give your time. Transcribe a page, read a reel, walk a cemetery with a camera. Every finding is marked with its finder.
    • Give the hunt a room. A library or society that hosts a hunt night gets the cases, the worksheets and the research desk for the evening.
    • Remember it. A gift in a will, to carry a hall of the hunt after its first sponsor. Ask us and we will send a line of wording.
