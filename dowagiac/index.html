    Cass County, Michigan

    Dowagiac, Michigan: its history, in the record

    Dowagiac grew where the Michigan Central Railroad crossed Dowagiac Creek in 1848, and was incorporated as a village in 1863 and a city in 1877. Its name is Potawatomi, from a word usually read as “fishing water.” It became the county’s manufacturing town: the Round Oak Stove works, founded by P. D. Beckwith in 1871, and the grain drills of the Dowagiac Manufacturing Company.

    The town at a glance

    • Founded: settled at the railroad crossing, 1848; village 1863; city 1877.
    • Name: Potawatomi, for the creek; several spellings in the early record.
    • In the county histories: Rogers (1875), Mathews (1882) and Glover (1906) each give the village its own chapter, with the first merchants, mills and churches named.
    • On the map: the 1860 wall map carries a plan of Dowagiac with its lots; lay it over today’s streets.
    • Industry: Round Oak stoves from 1871; at its height in the 1910s the works employed about 1,200 of the town’s 5,000 people.

    Where to look

    Dowagiac’s records are thick: a weekly press from the 1860s and a daily later, the Round Oak company papers, and city directories from the 1890s that list the heads of every household. The desk reads them for Cass County families as a matter of course. The records page links each source where it is held, and the glossary explains the words the old records use.
