Every clue is true.

Project Scroll is a history hunt played on paper. Old county histories, survey maps, death certificates and forgotten newspapers hold real mysteries, and every one of them can be solved without a shovel.

* And every answer is in the record.

The hunt

It began in one small Michigan county, with a lake that has no stream in and no stream out, a secret society from 1846, and a doctor who kept a bone. It is growing from there, one hall at a time.

Each mystery is posted on Facebook. Follow the clues, read the sources, and tell us what you find. When someone cracks one, we name them.

Start with Project Scroll on Facebook. The first door is in the pinned post.

The rules

  • Read the record. Every answer is in a book, on a map, or on a page.
  • Show your work. A name without the page it came from is only a guess.
  • Never dig, never go near a grave, and never set foot on anyone's land.
  • Be kind. These are real people, and their families are still here.

These are real cases from the Project Scroll desk. Every one began as a single question. Some are answered, some are still open, and we always say which. Now we're taking on yours.

  • A lost ancestorCensus rolls, death certificates, deeds, wills, church books and old newspapers, compared line by line. When the records disagree, we work out who was remembering, and when.
  • Who said it firstA family story, a famous quote or a local legend, traced back to the first time it was printed. Who wrote it down? Were they there? Who told them? What changed each time it was retold?
  • A forgotten placeA vanished mill, a schoolhouse, a cabin or a whole village, found again on old plat maps, surveyors' field notes and county atlases.
  • A legend worth testingGiant bones, underground rivers, buried treasure. We read the story against the records and tell you which parts hold up and which don't.
  • An heirloom no one can explainAn old photograph, a lodge badge, a deed, a letter or a piece of jewelry, traced through the people who owned it.
  • Lodges, churches and old societiesRosters, proceedings and minute books, where they survive and their keepers allow access, to learn who belonged, when, and what they did.
  • A second pair of eyesA family history, a book chapter or an article checked against its sources before it goes out into the world.

How we work

Every claim comes with its source. You'll know exactly where each fact came from, so you can check it yourself.

Disagreements are clues, not failures. When two records argue, we show you both and explain why.

Open questions stay open. If something might be true but can't be proven yet, we say so, and we keep looking instead of throwing it away.

No guarantees, just the record. Some questions have answers on paper and some don't. We'll tell you up front which kind yours looks like.

We research the past. No locating living people, no legal or title opinions, no appraisals. No digging, no trespassing, and care with every family's story.

What you get

A written report in plain English: what we found, where we found it, what is still open, and where to look next. Where the old books and maps are free online, we include the links so you can turn the pages yourself.
