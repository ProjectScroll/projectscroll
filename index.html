    Part II

    The hunt

    It began in Cassopolis, the seat of one small Michigan county, with a joke secret society from 1846, a lake that has no stream in and no stream out, and a doctor who kept a bone. It is growing from there, one hall at a time.

    1. Find a clue. Each mystery is posted on Facebook, with the first sources to open, and every case has its own page on the case board below.
    2. Search the record. Old books, maps and certificates, many of them free online.
    3. Post your answer. Put it in the comments, and say where you found it.
    4. Get named. When someone cracks one, the next post says who, and the name goes on the case board for good.

    Your first hunt, in five minutes

    1. Open case 1 on the board.
    2. Read the post that opened it: “Somebody lost a lodge.”
    3. Open Howard S. Rogers’s History of Cass County, from 1825 to 1875 (1875), pp. 253–254, free online, and ask who could have told the story.
    4. Open Alfred Mathews’s 1882 history, p. 174, and compare the two.
    5. Write down what each says on the hunter’s worksheet, then post your answer with the page.

    Ready? The first door is in the pinned post on Project Scroll’s Facebook page.

    Open the first door on Facebook

    The rules

    • Read the record. Every answer is in a book, on a map, or on a page.
    • Show your work. A name without the page it came from is only a guess.
    • Stay on paper. Never dig, never go near a grave, and never set foot on anyone’s land.
    • Be kind. These are real people, and their families are still here.

    Part V

    Questions people ask

    Does the hunt cost anything?

    No. The hunt is free to follow and free to play. All you need is curiosity and, now and then, a library card.

    Can you help with my own family’s mystery?

    Yes. Write to research@projectscroll.com, or send Project Scroll a message on Facebook, with what you know. We’ll tell you honestly what the records are likely to show before you commit to anything.

    How do I get in touch?

    Email research@projectscroll.com, or message the page on Facebook. Both reach the same desk. The form below lays out what to include. Expect a first reply within a few days; a full read of your records takes longer, and we say how long before we start.
