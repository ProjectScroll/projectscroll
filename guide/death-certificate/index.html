    How do I read a Michigan death certificate?

    A death certificate is two records in one. The doctor wrote down the death, at the time. Someone else, the informant, answered questions about a whole life, on the hardest day. Tap any numbered box to see who filled it in, and how far to trust it.

    The rule of thumb

    The top half (boxes 3 to 10) is memory: what the informant believed about a life. The bottom half (boxes 12 to 16) is the event: written by people who were there that week. When either half disagrees with another record, trust the bottom half first, and weigh the top half by who the informant was.

    Remember: the dead person was never the informant. Everything in the top half is someone else’s memory, given on the hardest day, so find the informant’s name on the certificate and ask how well they knew the life they were describing.

    Michigan’s certificates from 1897 to 1952 are free to view at the Archives of Michigan. Elsewhere, try FamilySearch or your state archives.
