    Outdoors & water

    Beaches, rivers, trails and orchards

    The valley is made of water: Lake Michigan on one side, the St. Joseph River through the middle, and lakes in every township. Here are the public beaches, parks, rail-trails and water trails, the u-pick orchards and the wine trail, with when to go, what it costs and whether the dog can come.

    Beaches and lake access 13

    Lake Michigan’s sand and the inland lakes’ swimming beaches. Michigan state parks need a Recreation Passport on the car; Indiana state parks charge at the gate.

    • Beach / state park · Sawyer, MI

      Warren Dunes State Park

      Lake Michigan beach under some of the tallest dunes on the shore; swim in the buoyed area, climb Tower Hill, or walk six miles of wooded trails. Paddleboard and kayak rentals in summer.

      When: Open all year; beach season Memorial Day to Labor Day; cross-country skiing listed as a winter activity. Phone 269-426-4013.

      Cost: Michigan Recreation Passport required for vehicle entry (nonresident day pass $12, annual $42; residents $15 with plate renewal).

      Dogs: Yes, on a 6-foot leash, on the beach north of the northernmost swim buoy (about 2.5 miles of shoreline).

      Access: Three accessible restroom buildings in the beach day-use area; three 100-foot accessible walkways toward the water (they stop short of the water's edge); two beach wheelchairs to borrow at the ranger station.

      The story: Businessman Edward K. Warren bought the dunes and beach as a conservation measure; the land became a state park in 1930. Source

      12032 Red Arrow Highway, Sawyer, MI 49125

      Official page · Directions

    • Beach / state park / dunes · Stevensville, MI

      Grand Mere State Park

      Hike over wooded dunes to about half a mile of quiet Lake Michigan shoreline, or fish for bluegill on South and Middle lakes; a fine spring birding spot.

      When: Open all year; snowshoeing in winter. Vault toilets and a picnic pavilion by the main lot.

      Cost: Michigan Recreation Passport (vehicle entry).

      Dogs: Yes, on a 6-foot leash, along the half mile of Lake Michigan shoreline.

      The story: Its three inland lakes, remnants of five, formed as the glaciers retreated; the area was named a National Natural Landmark in 1968. Source

      No street address published by the DNR (directions link only); off Thornton Drive, Stevensville area — verify

      Official page · Directions

    • Beach / state park · South Haven, MI

      Van Buren State Park

      A sandy Lake Michigan beach backed by high dunes, with a buoyed swim area, a pet-friendly beach just north of it, and a paved spur to the Van Buren Trail for biking.

      When: Open all year; paddlesport rentals from the Friday before Memorial Day through Labor Day. Phone 269-637-2788.

      Cost: Michigan Recreation Passport (vehicle entry).

      Dogs: Yes, leashed (6 feet), on most of the shoreline except the buoyed swim area; designated pet beach just north of it.

      Access: Track chair to borrow; accessible restroom, parking and a paved walkway to the beach at the swim area; ADA campsites.

      The story: Established in 1966 after the state bought 167 acres from the Harry LaBar Drake family in 1965. Source

      23960 Ruggles Road, South Haven, MI 49090

      Official page · Directions

    • Beach / county park · St. Joseph, MI

      Silver Beach County Park

      A busy, lifeguarded Lake Michigan beach below the bluff with a walk out the South Pier, volleyball courts, playgrounds, and kayak and paddleboard rentals at the north end.

      When: Open dawn to dusk year-round; restrooms early May to mid-October; lifeguards early June to mid-August. Phone 269-982-0533.

      Cost: Vehicle entry May–September: $8 Berrien County residents, $15 nonresidents; $40 annual Berrien County parks permit. Walk-ins free. Michigan Recreation Passport not accepted.

      Dogs: Leashed on sidewalks only; no pets on the sand or in the water.

      Access: Barrier-free walkway from the lot along the beachfront to the pavilions and South Pier; free beach wheelchair and beach walker in summer, first come first served (ID required, must be pushed).

      The story: Silver Beach became a Berrien County park in the early 1990s. Source

      101 Broad Street, St. Joseph, MI 49085

      Official page · Directions

    • Beach / city park · St. Joseph, MI

      Tiscornia Park (Tiscornia Beach)

      A 16-acre family beach on the north side of the St. Joseph River mouth with restrooms and a picnic pavilion; walk the North Pier to the two lighthouses at sunset.

      When: Parking passes required April 1–October 31; lighthouse tours offered between Memorial Day and Labor Day weekends.

      Cost: Paid parking (meter by the hour or day; $40 season city parks pass) April–October. Rates not published on the page we could read.

      Dogs: Leashed dogs on the sidewalk and pier only.

      The story: A light station has stood at the river mouth since 1832; the present North Pier inner and outer lights date from 1906–1907 and are on the National Register of Historic Places. Source

      80 Ridgeway Street, St. Joseph, MI 49085

      Official page · Directions

      The story: In 1917 John Nellis Klock and his wife Carrie bought the lakeshore from E. K. Warren and deeded it to the City of Benton Harbor in memory of their daughter Jean. Source

    Parks, forests and nature centers 16

    Old-growth woods, dunes, a dark-sky park, botanical gardens and nature centers with trails for every season.

    • State park / old-growth forest · Three Oaks (near), MI

      Warren Woods State Park

      A quiet one-mile trail along the Galien River through towering beech and sugar maple, with a footbridge over the river; a favorite in spring wildflower season and peak fall color.

      When: Open all year; the Elm Valley Road entrance is closed in winter, use the Warren Woods Road access. Vault toilet only.

      Cost: Michigan Recreation Passport (vehicle entry).

      Dogs: Not stated on the DNR page (state park rule: leashed, 6 feet).

      The story: Edward K. Warren began buying the woods in 1879 to preserve them; the stand, called the last climax beech-maple forest in Michigan, has been a National Natural Landmark since 1967. Source

      Warren Woods Road trailhead (Elm Valley Road entrance closed in winter); no street address published

      Official page · Directions

    • County park / dark-sky park · Vandalia, MI

      Dr. T. K. Lawless Park

      Cass County's big all-season park: seven miles of hiking trails, ten miles of mountain-bike singletrack, disc golf and a paved one-mile accessible trail by day, and some of the darkest skies in southern Michigan by night, with scheduled public sky-viewing nights.

      When: Open year-round; hours posted at the park. Winter: seven miles of groomed cross-country ski trail and inner-tube sledding (tubes provided).

      Cost: Admission $3 Cass County residents, $5 nonresidents.

      Dogs: Not stated on the county page — check Cass County Parks rules.

      Access: Paved total-access trail (1 mile); modern restrooms; large parking area.

      The story: Accepted as an International Dark Sky Park in January 2020. Source

      15122 Monkey Run Street, Vandalia, MI 49095

      Official page · Directions

    • County park / nature center · New Carlisle, IN

      Bendix Woods County Park

      Wooded trails, a 6.5-mile mountain-bike trail, a nature center in the old clubhouse, maple-sugaring in late winter, and the famous pine trees planted to spell STUDEBAKER.

    • State park / lake beach · North Liberty, IN

      Potato Creek State Park

      Swim at the Worster Lake beach, rent a canoe, kayak or rowboat (electric motors only), ride the paved bike trail or the 7.4-mile mountain-bike loop, and hike the woods and prairie.

      When: Daily 7 a.m.–11 p.m. Winter: cross-country skiing (no rentals) and a tubing hill.

      Cost: Indiana state park entrance: $7 per Indiana vehicle, $15 out-of-state; annual pass $50 resident / $120 nonresident.

      Dogs: Not stated on the park page (Indiana state parks generally allow leashed pets outside beaches and buildings — verify).

      Access: Motorized trail wheelchair to borrow; ADA sidewalk from the lot to the water's edge.

      The story: Dedicated as Potato Creek Recreation Area on June 6, 1977, the year its dam created Worster Lake, named for conservationist Darcy Worster; it became a state park in 1983. Source

    • Nature center / preserve · Benton Harbor (Benton Center), MI

      Sarett Nature Center

      Nine miles of trails through river bottomland, woods and wetland, boardwalks, a treetop walkway, a nature play area and a summer Butterfly House; a fine place to bird.

      When: Trails dawn to dusk daily; building Tuesday–Saturday 10–5, Sunday 1–5, closed Monday; Butterfly House May–September. Winter: cross-country ski and snowshoe rentals. Phone 269-927-4832.

      Access: An accessible trail; trail accessibility vehicles available (see sarett.org/trailaccessibility).

      The story: Founded in 1963 when Elizabeth and William Vawter bought 130 acres and Michigan Audubon agreed to manage it; named for the naturalist and poet Lew Sarett. Source

      2300 N. Benton Center Road, Benton Harbor, MI 49022

    Trails to walk and ride 7

    Rail-trails on old railroad beds and riverwalks along the St. Joseph and Elkhart rivers.

    • Trail (rail-trail) · South Haven to Kalamazoo, MI

      Kal-Haven Trail State Park

      Ride or walk about 34 miles of crushed limestone between Lake Michigan and Kalamazoo through farms, woods and small trail towns; a hybrid or gravel bike suits it better than a road bike.

      When: Year-round; cross-country skiing allowed (not groomed); snowmobiles Dec. 1–March 31. Phone 269-637-2788.

      Cost: No trail-use fee stated (the county trail pass was dropped in 2011); Recreation Passport for state park vehicle entry where applicable.

      Dogs: Not stated on the DNR page (state rule: leashed, 6 feet).

      Access: Limestone/slag surface.

      The story: Built on the bed of the Kalamazoo and South Haven Railroad (1870s), abandoned in 1973; the trail opened August 15, 1989. Source

      Trailheads in South Haven and Kalamazoo, with access at Grand Junction, Bloomingdale and Alamo

      Official page · Directions

    • Trail (rail-trail) · South Haven to Hartford, MI

      Van Buren Trail State Park

      A 14-mile former rail line: two paved miles from South Haven to 16th Avenue, then gravel to Hartford suited to mountain bikes and horses; links to the Kal-Haven Trail.

      When: Year-round; skiing allowed (not groomed); snowmobiles on 13 miles Dec. 1–March 31.

      Access: First two miles paved.

      The story: Follows a former railroad right-of-way. Source

      South Haven to Hartford; spur from Van Buren State Park

      Official page

    • Trail (rail-trail) · Goshen – Middlebury – Shipshewana, IN

    Paddling and the rivers 9

    Water trails on the St. Joseph, Dowagiac, Paw Paw and Galien rivers, and the liveries and launches that put you on them.

    • Paddling / whitewater course · South Bend, IN · Closed this season

      East Race Waterway

      A Class II whitewater channel in the heart of downtown where the public can raft (gear provided) or bring their own kayak.

      When: CLOSED for the 2026 season for updates. Normally June 1–Labor Day, weekends.

      Cost: Normally a $20 day pass with unlimited rides; 54-inch minimum height.

      The story: Dug as an industrial raceway in the late 1800s, later filled in, re-excavated and opened in 1984 as the first man-made whitewater rafting course in North America. Source

      Downtown South Bend along the east bank of the St. Joseph River

      Official page

    • Paddling / water trail · Niles to St. Joseph, MI

      St. Joseph River Water Trail (Michigan)

      About 67 river miles from Niles to Lake Michigan with good fishing; plan portages at the dams in Niles, Buchanan and Berrien Springs, and watch for big boats and freighters in the working harbor at the mouth.

      When: Spring through fall; check flows and weather.

      Cost: Free (Recreation Passport for vehicles at state launches).

      Access: Varies by launch.

      Access sites in Niles, Buchanan, Berrien Springs, Benton Harbor and St. Joseph (interactive map on the trail page)

      Official page

    • Paddling / water trail · North of Dowagiac to Niles, MI

      Dowagiac River Water Trail

      When: Spring through fall; stay off after heavy rain. USGS gage: waterdata.usgs.gov site 04101800.

      Cost: Free.

      Access: Varies.

      The story: The Pucker Street dam has been removed. Source

      Access points on the trail-page map; last access before the St. Joseph is the DNR site on M-139 just north of Niles

    Fall color and winter

    Fall color drives

    • Red Arrow Highway (West Michigan Pike). From St. Joseph take Main Street south to Lakeshore Drive and Red Arrow Highway through Stevensville, Bridgman, Sawyer, Harbert, Lakeside and Union Pier to New Buffalo
    • Blue Star Highway (West Michigan Pike north). From St. Joseph north on tree-lined M-63 to Blue Star Highway along Lake Michigan through Benton Harbor and Covert toward South Haven
    • St. Joseph River Tour. From St. Joseph follow Niles Avenue/M-63 to Scottdale, then M-139 and Red Bud Trail to Buchanan
    • South Berrien wine-country loop. I-94 to Exit 12 (Sawyer); east on Sawyer Road, Flynn Road, Browntown Road, Hills Road and Lemon Creek Road, then Red Arrow Highway to Bridgman and Union Pier
    • Madron Lake Road, Buchanan. A short wooded road near Buchanan named by the County Road Association of Michigan
    • Fred Russ Forest Park tour (Cass County). I-94 Exit 56 (Decatur), M-51 south, left on Decatur Road, left on Marcellus Highway to the forest park
    • Warren Woods State Park walk. Peak color in the old beech-maple forest off Warren Woods Road near Three Oaks

    Color usually peaks around the middle of October. Routes from Visit Southwest Michigan’s Fall Color Guide and the County Road Association of Michigan.

    Before you go

    • Passes. Michigan Recreation Passport: $15 resident annual with plate renewal (2-year $29); nonresident $12 daily, $42 annual; covers vehicle entry to all 103 state parks and recreation areas and nearly 1,000 boating access sites. Source: https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/buy-and-apply/recreation-passport
    • Indiana state parks. Indiana state parks: $7 per Indiana vehicle, $15 out-of-state per day at most parks; annual $50 resident, $120 nonresident. Source: https://secure.in.gov/dnr/state-parks/rates-and-fees/entrance-fees
    • Public land only. Every place here is open to the public. Please keep to paths and posted hours, and leave private shores and farm fields alone.
    • Lake Michigan. Watch the beach flags and the forecast: rip currents along the piers are real. Never walk the piers in high waves.
