What we research

  • A lost ancestorCensus rolls, death certificates, deeds, wills, church books and old newspapers, compared line by line. When the records disagree, we work out who was remembering, and when.
  • Who said it firstA family story, a famous quote or a local legend, traced back to the first time it was printed. Who wrote it down? Were they there? Who told them? What changed each time it was retold?
  • A forgotten placeA vanished mill, a schoolhouse, a cabin or a whole village, found again on old plat maps, surveyors’ field notes and county atlases.
  • A local legend worth testingGiant bones, underground rivers, buried treasure. We read the story against the records and tell you which parts hold up and which don’t.
  • An heirloom no one can explainAn old photograph, a lodge badge, a deed, a letter or a piece of jewelry, traced through the people who owned it.
  • Lodges, churches and old societiesRosters, proceedings and minute books, where they survive and their keepers allow access, to learn who belonged, when, and what they did.
  • A family history or registerEvery descendant of one couple, or every ancestor of one person, built from the records, with every statement marked and every gap shown honestly. See a specimen.
  • Is the family story true?The Ellis Island name change, the famous ancestor, the three brothers who came over together. We find where the story began and what the records say. Test a family legend.
  • A second pair of eyesA family history, a book chapter or an article checked against its sources before it goes out into the world.

Imaging, design, 3D and video

The same care, turned on pictures, landscapes and the record itself. Everything you send is handled gently and returned as you sent it.

  • Digital imagingCareful, high-resolution images of photographs, letters, certificates, maps, Bibles and heirlooms, so the originals can be put safely away and the copies shared.
  • Imaging analysisFaded ink brought back, damaged or water-stained pages made readable again, and the small details in an old photograph studied and compared: the clothes, the buildings, the signs in a window.
  • Image creation and restorationTorn and faded photographs repaired, old maps redrawn clean, and new illustrations made from what the record describes, always labeled as new work.
  • Graphic designFamily-history books and their covers, ancestral charts, reunion and memorial pieces, and anything else that deserves to be laid out well.
  • Web designWebsites and pages for families, historical societies, museums and small businesses: designed, written and built to be easy to read, quick to load on a phone, and easy to keep up to date. This site is an example.
  • PhotographyHeirlooms, documents and family pieces photographed properly, and places that matter to a family history.
  • Video productionShort family-history films, recorded interviews with the people who still remember, and documentaries built from the record and the photographs.
  • 3D LiDAR imagingLandscapes modeled in three dimensions from public LiDAR elevation surveys, which show old roads, field lines, mill races and shorelines that are hard to see on the ground. Made from the data, with no need to set foot on anyone’s land.
  • 3D animationFly-throughs of a landscape, and buildings or places shown as the record describes them, every reconstruction labeled as a reconstruction.
  • Living documentsResearch papers, timelines and family records that keep growing as new evidence turns up, with every change dated, so the latest version is always the true one.

Is my research question answerable?

Four quick questions for an honest first read. Nothing you choose leaves this page.

  1. Do you know at least one full name?
  2. Do you know a place, even just a county or a town?
  3. Do you know a rough date, within twenty years or so?
  4. Did it happen before about 1950?

What happens when you bring us one

  1. Tell us what you know. A name, a place, rough dates, and whatever the family says. Our message form lays it out for you to send by email or Facebook Messenger.
  2. Get an honest first read. We tell you what the records are likely to show, and what they probably can’t, before you commit to anything.
  3. Agree on the question. One clear question, and where we will look to answer it.
  4. We search the record. Certificates, deeds, censuses, old newspapers, county histories and maps, compared line by line.
  5. You get the report. Plain English, every statement marked, every source listed, and what is still open.

For studio work it is simpler: tell us what you have and what you would like made, we tell you honestly what can be done, and you get the finished files, with your originals returned as you sent them.
