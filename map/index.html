    The map room

    The 1860 wall map

    In 1860 Geil, Harley & Siverd of Philadelphia engraved a great wall map of Cass, Van Buren and Berrien counties, Michigan: every township, the farms and the names of the people on them, with little town plans and pictures of houses around the border. The original is at the Library of Congress. Here you can walk over every inch of it.

    Sanborn fire-insurance maps, building by building

    From the 1880s the Sanborn Map Company drew every business block for the insurers: each building’s outline, what it was built of (pink for brick, yellow for wood), how many storeys, and what went on inside: a livery, a hall, a hotel. Twelve towns on the trail have them. Choose a town and a sheet, and it opens in the viewer above.

    The sheets are the Library of Congress’s scans, shown from Wikimedia Commons. Public domain.

    Old postcards on the map

    121 old postcards and photographs of 29 towns, each pinned where it was taken. Press a town to see its pictures, then open any one in the postcards gallery.

    The map loads here.

    Things to look for

    • The small town plans along the top and left side, with streets, blocks and the names of buildings.
    • The business directories printed in the margins, listing who kept which shop or hotel.
    • The engraved houses around the border: the proudest farms and homes of 1860.
    • Every farm, marked with its owner’s name and acreage. Find your family’s name on the land in 1860, then look for the same name in the deed books.

    More maps in the room

    The county, township by township, about 1928

    All fifteen of the county’s townships from a plat book of about 1928, each farm with its owner’s name and acreage, two generations after the 1860 map. Open a sheet and read the names; then look for the same names on the wall map above. Penn Township is laid over its 1860 self on a slider on the Cassopolis page, with ten families to find.

    Four families who stayed in Ontwa
