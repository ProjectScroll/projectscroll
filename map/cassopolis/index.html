    Then and now

    Cassopolis, 1860 and today

    The 1860 wall map has a little plan of Cassopolis, the county seat, with every block, lot and street the engraver knew. Here it is laid over the streets of today. Fade between the two, or drag a line across and see one beside the other.

    Other towns: Niles · Dowagiac · Paw Paw

    Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom. The old plan was lined up by hand on street corners that still carry their 1860 names, so it is close but not exact: expect it to be out by a few yards in places.

    Things to look for

    • State Street and Broadway. The heart of the 1860 town. Find the Court House and the Jail just north of State, then fade to today.
    • Timber Street, between O’Keefe and East. Look for it on today’s map.
    • The Union School at the top of the plan, beside the State Road.
    • The Tannery and the Cemetery on the west side of town, and the State Road running north.
    • The names on the lots: Tompkins, Martin, Histed, Read, Jones and the rest. Each one is a lead to a deed.

    How this was made

    The plan was cut from the Library of Congress copy of the wall map. Nineteen street corners that appear on both maps (Broadway, Rowland, O’Keefe and East, where they meet York, State, Jefferson, Water and South) were matched by measurement, and the plan was scaled to fit them. On average the old corners land within about five yards of the new ones.

    One label has been painted out of this copy so that it does not spoil the hunt. Nothing else has been changed.
