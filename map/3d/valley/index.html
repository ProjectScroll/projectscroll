    The valley in 3D

    The St. Joseph River Valley

    All 12,209 square kilometres that drain to the St. Joseph River, from the hills past Hillsdale to Lake Michigan, built from the U.S. Geological Survey’s laser-made elevation of the ground. The river and its branches are drawn in gold. Three lost towns are hidden in the hills: find all three.

    What you are looking at

    • The whole watershed. The bright land is every acre whose rain ends in the St. Joseph River: parts of fifteen counties in Michigan and Indiana. The darker land around it drains elsewhere: to the Kalamazoo, the Maumee, the Kankakee, or straight to Lake Michigan.
    • The shape of the ground. The heights come from the U.S. Geological Survey’s 3D Elevation Program, which in this country is made mostly from laser surveys flown county by county. Here it is sampled every 100 metres, and the heights are raised (×15 to start) because the valley falls only about 200 metres from its eastern hills to the lake. Use the slider to flatten it or raise it further.
    • What the ice left. The long ridges and knobby hills are moraines, left where the glaciers stood and melted, about 15,000 years ago. The flat, open plains between them are outwash, sand and gravel spread by the meltwater. The river wanders through them both.
    • The gold lights. Three are the sites of lost towns from the county histories. Tap each one. Find all three and the valley is yours: there is a badge for it.

    Sources: U.S. Geological Survey, 3D Elevation Program, bare-earth elevation (public domain); watershed boundary from the USGS Watershed Boundary Dataset (HUC 04050001, St. Joseph); rivers and towns from OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors. 3D drawing by three.js. See also Cassopolis in LiDAR, where every laser point is shown.
