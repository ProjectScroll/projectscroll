    Cassopolis in LiDAR

    The county seat, laser by laser

    In 2015 an aircraft flew over Cass County firing laser pulses at the ground, hundreds of thousands a second, and timing each echo. Every dot below is one of those echoes: 775,557 of them in the blocks around the courthouse, every one the survey kept, and 759,226 more across the country around. Around them lies the land the same survey measured, from Stone Lake to Diamond Lake, about six kilometres across. Turn it, tilt it, and lay the 1860 town plan over the real ground.

    • Buildings, 193,991 points
    • Trees, 1,340,792 points
    • The bare ground, with a contour every two metres
    • Stone Lake and Diamond Lake

    What you are looking at

    • Real survey points, not a drawing. Each dot is a laser return recorded by the U.S. Geological Survey’s 2015 flight over Cass County, placed to within a few centimetres. Roof shapes, chimneys and the crowns of single trees are all there because the laser hit them.
    • The ground. The survey marks which returns struck bare earth. Those make the surface underneath, a model of the land with the buildings and trees taken off. It rises about 14 metres across these blocks.
    • Buildings and trees. The survey does not say which of the other points are roofs and which are leaves, so we sorted them: a laser pulse that hits a roof comes back once; one that hits a tree comes back several times, from leaves, branches and the ground below. Flat, single echoes in a patch the size of a building are coloured gold. A few porches and dense evergreens may be the wrong colour.
    • The lakes and the land around. Stone Lake lies just west of the courthouse, and Diamond Lake, with Grand Island in it, a short way east. The wider ground comes from the same 2015 flight, as the survey’s bare-earth elevation: the shape of the land every six metres or so, and its light and shade from a grid one and a half metres apart, sharp enough to show field edges, ditches and old roadbeds. The lakes are drawn flat at their surface, because the survey does not see through water. Buildings and trees are shown only in the town blocks, where every point is kept.
    • The 1860 plan. Press 1860 plan to lay the town plan from the 1860 county wall map on the ground. Its west part reaches into these blocks. Compare it with the flat then-and-now page.
    • The 1909 fire map. Press 1909 fire map to lay the Sanborn fire-insurance map of January 1909 on the ground: every building downtown, in pink for brick and yellow for wood, from the courthouse to the hotel. We fitted its two sheets to today’s street corners; they line up to within about a metre.
    • Lake depths. Press Lake depths to see the bottom of Diamond Lake drawn in lines every ten feet, from the state’s lake survey. The deepest point is 64 feet, east of the island.
    • Story pins. The gold pins are the stops of our walking tour of Cassopolis, from the site of the first courthouse to the bank corner, and the island in Diamond Lake. Press one for a line of its history.
    • Guided tour. Press Guided tour and the view flies itself from Stone Lake to the courthouse square and out to Diamond Lake. Touch the model to stop it.
    • Sun and shadows. Move the Sun slider through a midsummer day, from six in the morning to eight at night, and every roof and tree in the town blocks casts its shadow on the ground. The shadows are worked out from the laser points themselves.
    • Tap for a reading. Tap or click the bare ground anywhere, and the model tells you how high it stands above sea level and how far it is from the courthouse.
    • Buildings and Trees. Each button hides or shows its points, so you can see the bare land under the town, or the trees alone.
    • Height ×3. The town is fairly level, so tripling the heights makes the rise of the land easier to see. The buildings stretch too.
