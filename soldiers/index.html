    Soldiers of the valley

    Company D, Sixth Michigan Infantry

    In the summer of 1861 a company was raised at Dowagiac, the second organized in Cass County. Here are its 85 men as the county history printed them in 1882: who they were, how they served, and how 19 of them did not come home.

    The company’s war

    • Raised at Dowagiac, summer 1861, under Capt. Charles E. Clarke, with Frederick J. Clarke as first lieutenant. The regiment gathered at Kalamazoo and left for the East on 30 August 1861, 944 strong. Mathews, p. 117
    • Baltimore, then the Mississippi. After garrison duty at Baltimore it went to New Orleans and the lower Mississippi, fought at Baton Rouge on 5 August 1862, and in the assault on Port Hudson on 27 May 1863, where Lieutenant Frederick Clarke was killed. pp. 117–118
    • Heavy artillery. After Port Hudson the regiment became heavy artillery, served at Fort Morgan and in the siege of Mobile, and was paid off and discharged at Jackson, Michigan, on 5 September 1865. p. 118

    Of the men below, 4 were killed or died of wounds and 15 died of disease: at Baltimore, New Orleans, Baton Rouge, Carrollton and, most of all, in the hot summer at Port Hudson. Disease killed more soldiers than battle in this war, and this company is no exception.

    Reading the abbreviations

    • com. commissioned
    • e. enlisted
    • dis. discharged
    • m. o. mustered out
    • vet. re-enlisted as a veteran
    • prom. promoted
    • trans. transferred
    • U. S. C. T. United States Colored Troops

    A man in this roll is a lead. His service record and pension file at the National Archives can say far more: where he was wounded, who his widow was, where he lived after the war. A government headstone with a sunk shield marks many of these men’s graves; see reading old gravestones.
