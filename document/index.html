    One page of Rogers, 1875

    Every month we put one real page on the table and read it slowly. This month: page 253 of Howard S. Rogers’s History of Cass County, from 1825 to 1875, the first time the joke lodge of 1846 was ever printed, with the man who told it to Rogers only a footnote away.

    What it is

    Rogers was Cass County’s first historian. His book came out in 1875, fifty years after the county’s first land entries, while people who had seen 1846 were still alive to be asked. That is the book’s value and its danger at once: it is close to the events, and it is made of what people remembered.

    How to read it

    • Read past the quotation. Rogers quotes a long passage and then adds a few words of his own. The words after the closing quotation mark are where a careful reader slows down.
    • Watch the punctuation. On the next page a single question mark sits inside a parenthesis. A question mark in a county history is a historian telling you, as plainly as he dared, that he is not sure.
    • Find the teller. The passage is a quotation, and Rogers was not there in 1846. Seven years later Alfred Mathews reprinted the passage and put the teller’s name in a footnote: James M. Shepard, who was three years old in 1846. Who told him?
    • Count what can be counted. Two hundred badges, one ball, one winter. Each number is a thing that might have left a record somewhere else: a newspaper, a bill, a diary, a badge in a drawer.

    Read it yourself

    The whole book is free online in the University of Michigan’s county-histories collection: Rogers, History of Cass County, from 1825 to 1875. Go to pages 253–254. Then compare Mathews, 1882, page 174, where the same story is printed again, and notice what is missing.

    This page is the opening clue of Case 1. If you read it closely enough, you may not need the rest.
