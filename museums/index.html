    Cass County, Michigan

    • Society · Cassopolis

      Cass County Historical Society

      The county historical society. It runs the Newton House museum together with the county and publishes books on Cass County history.

      For researchers: Its publications are sold through the Cass District Library Local History Branch.

      Email ccmihistoricalsociety@gmail.com.

      P.O. Box 21, Cassopolis, MI 49031 (mailing)

      Their website ›

    • Library history room · Cassopolis

      Cass District Library – Local History Branch

      A branch library given over to Cass County history and genealogy.

      For researchers: Plat maps, county newspapers on microfilm (Cassopolis, Dowagiac, Edwardsburg, Marcellus, Niles titles), obituary indexes 1998–2025, marriage, death and divorce records on microfilm, cemetery and rural-school guides, yearbooks and a vertical file.

      Mon–Thu 9–4; closed Fri–Sun. Phone 269-357-7823.

      145 N. Broadway, Cassopolis, MI 49031

      Their website ›

    • Museum · Cassopolischeck before you go

      Pioneer Log Cabin Museum

      A log cabin museum in Cassopolis, kept by volunteers, holding pioneer-era objects from Cass County.

      Open at times through the season; see the museum’s website.

      400 S. Broadway St., Cassopolis, MI

      Their website ›

    • Historic house · Decatur (Volinia Township)

      Newton House

      A historic farmhouse museum with permanent and changing displays on daily life from 1844 to 1930, when the Newton family lived there.

      For researchers: Several rooms are set aside for reading and research on the family, the surrounding woods and local history; it also keeps documents, family histories and artifacts.

      Open houses on the first Sunday of each month, June to September, 1:00–4:30 pm.

      20689 Marcellus Hwy, Decatur, MI 49045

      Their website ›

    • Museum · Dowagiac

      Dowagiac Area History Museum

      A free museum with about 6,000 square feet of exhibits on the history of Dowagiac, Cass County and the surrounding area, including the Round Oak stove story and an Orphan Train exhibit.

      For researchers: Online collections database; ask the museum about research use (269-783-2560, info@dowagiacmuseum.info).

      Tue–Fri 10–5, Sat 10–2. Free.

      201 E. Division St., Dowagiac, MI 49047

      Their website ›

    • Library history room · Dowagiac

      Dowagiac District Library – Genealogy & Local History

      The public library for Dowagiac, with books, microfilm and digital resources for genealogy and local history.

      For researchers: Questions to genealogy@dowagiacdl.org.

      Mon/Wed/Fri 9–5:30, Tue/Thu 9–7, Sat 9–2.

      211 Commercial St., Dowagiac, MI 49047

      Their website ›

    • Museum · Edwardsburg

      Edwardsburg Area History Museum

      A community history museum in a donated house on Main Street, with displays and talks on Edwardsburg-area history.

      For researchers: Contact the museum about research (269-663-3005, edwardsburgmuseum@yahoo.com).

      Open mid-April to mid-December; Tue–Fri 1–4, Sat 11–2.

      26818 Main Street, Edwardsburg, MI 49112

      Their website ›

    • Historic house · Vandalia

      Bonine House

      A restored nineteenth-century home in Vandalia, open as a museum with docent-led visits. It tells the story of Cass County's part in the Underground Railroad, and its seasonal events include a Christmas open house.

      May to October, Mon–Fri 2–5 pm; private tours by arrangement.

      18970 M-60, Vandalia, MI 49095

    Before you go

    • Check the hours. Many of these places are run by volunteers and open only some days or some seasons. Their own websites have the latest.
    • Researchers, write ahead. Archives and research rooms often keep their records off the floor. A note a week before lets them have your boxes ready.
    • Bring what you know. Names, dates, the township, and a copy of anything you found here. Staff can do far more with a good question.
    • Know of a place we missed? Tell us and we will add it.

    Every place on this page was checked against its own website, or a city, county, library or university page about it, in October 2026. Places marked “check before you go” could not be confirmed online. Hours change: always look at the place’s own site before travelling.
