    Cass County, Michigan

    Cassopolis, Michigan: its history, in the record

    Cassopolis is the county seat of Cass County, Michigan, platted in 1831 and named for Lewis Cass, then governor of Michigan Territory. This page gathers what Project Scroll has opened so far on the village and its county: the maps, the county histories, the certificates and the newspapers, with a link to each record where it is held.

    The village at a glance

    • Founded: platted 1831; chosen as county seat the same year, a compromise between rival settlements.
    • Named for: Lewis Cass (1782–1866), governor of Michigan Territory 1813–31, later Secretary of War and of State.
    • First published histories: Howard S. Rogers, History of Cass County, from 1825 to 1875 (Cassopolis, 1875); Alfred Mathews, History of Cass County, Michigan (Chicago, 1882); L. H. Glover, A Twentieth Century History of Cass County (Chicago, 1906).
    • On the map: the 1860 Geil & Harley wall map of Cass County, held by the Library of Congress, carries a plan of the village with lot numbers and owners; you can lay it over today’s streets, and the county’s township sheets of about 1928 are in the map room.
    • Vital records: Michigan death certificates from 1897 are free to read at the Archives of Michigan; here is how to read one.

    Next door, 1860 and 1928

    Penn Township is the ground just east of the village, with Diamond Lake in its south-west corner and Vandalia on the railroad. Here it is twice: as the 1860 wall map engraved it, and as a plat book drew it about 1928. The square mile sections are the same, so the two line up. Slide across and watch sixty-eight years of owners change hands.

    A challenge

    Find the families who stayed

    Ten surnames are written on this township in both years: on a farm in 1860, and still on the land about 1928. Find them. Slide the map, read the names, and type each one you find.

      The hotel corner, thirty years on

      In 1860 the engraver wrote one word on the block by the court house: HOTEL. Twenty-four years later the Sanborn Map Company drew the same corner building by building, for the insurers. The court house and post office face Broadway, the county jail and the sheriff’s house stand behind them, and across the street stands a hotel under a name the 1860 directory never lists. Is it on the ground where the 1860 hotel stood? Lay the two maps side by side and decide for yourself.
