    Lessons from the desk

    No. 1 · 17 October 2026

    One Letter, Two Certificates

    One letter. That’s all it was. A single middle initial, and it nearly turned one man into two.

    2

    The case

    A pioneer died in Michigan in 1912. The old county histories give him a plain first and last name. One of them adds “Senior.”

    His own death certificate, filed about a month after he died, gives the same plain name. No initial at all.

    3

    Then we found his son’s death certificate, from 1935. In the box for the father’s name, whoever answered the questions gave him a middle initial: the same one the son carried.

    So which is right?

    4

    Then it got stranger

    His birthday: his death certificate and one of the county histories are two days apart.

    His father’s birthplace: the certificate names one state. That same history names another.

    Nobody lied. Each one is somebody’s memory.

    5

    What this teaches

    Every record was written by someone. A death certificate tells you what the informant believed, on a hard day.

    One wrong initial can merge two men or split one, and send you chasing somebody else’s family for a month.

    6

    Four rules

    1. Ask who gave the information, and when.
    2. Keep looking. Records are weighed, not counted.
    3. Write down both versions. Never quietly “fix” one.
    4. Call it settled only when the evidence settles it.
    7

    Where it stands

    The case is still open. The next witnesses: a newspaper notice from the days after his death, his probate file, and the cemetery’s records, read on paper.

    More lessons will be bound into this book as they are posted.
